**The framing — four levels of uncertainty**

## Not all uncertainty is the same problem

A refresher on the Walker / Courtney ladder — because everything in this workbench hangs on one distinction: uncertainty you can buy down with information, versus uncertainty that only the world can resolve. The Samsung verdict turns on the second kind.

---

## The ladder — and where the wall sits

An ascending staircase of four levels, with **THE WALL** standing between Level 2 and Level 3:

- **LEVEL 1** — A clear enough future
- **LEVEL 2** — Alternate futures, with probabilities
- — THE WALL —
- **LEVEL 3** — A few plausible futures — no credible probabilities
- **LEVEL 4** — True ambiguity — unknown unknowns

*Levels 1–2: analysis converges on an answer. Levels 3–4: analysis can only map the territory. The wall between 2 and 3 is where the method must change.*

---

## Level 1 · A clear enough future

**The question:** What will happen?

A single forecast is accurate enough to make the decision. Residual uncertainty is noise around one trajectory — wide enough to need error bars, narrow enough not to change the answer.

**Key characteristics**
- Stable environment, abundant history
- Well-understood causal structure
- Errors are symmetric and bounded

**The right tools**
- Point forecasts
- DCF / NPV on a single case
- Sensitivity as error bars

**In this case:** Will GEO satellite latency stay in the 600–800 ms band? Yes — it is set by orbital altitude and the speed of light. Physics does not negotiate.

---

## Level 2 · Alternate futures, with probabilities

**The question:** What are the odds?
*(standard analysis lives here — Innovera today)*

The future forks into a few discrete outcomes — and the odds attached to each are credible, because they come from data, structure, or a process you can study. This is risk in the classic sense: countable outcomes, defensible probabilities.

**Key characteristics**
- Outcome set is enumerable
- Probabilities are estimable, not invented
- More analysis genuinely shrinks the uncertainty

**The right tools**
- Decision trees, expected value
- Monte Carlo over known distributions
- Real options on discrete events

**In this case:** What will a user terminal cost at 1M-unit volume? A bottom-up BOM study yields a defensible range with odds. This is exactly the uncertainty the memo's Phase-1 budget is designed to buy down.

---

## Level 3 · A few plausible futures — no credible probabilities

**The question:** Under which futures does this decision survive?
*(the defensible territory — where this workbench operates)*

You can still bound the future with a handful of scenarios — but there is no defensible basis for odds. The outcome hangs on choices other actors have not yet made. Assigning probabilities here doesn't add rigor; it launders guesswork into math. This is where deep uncertainty begins.

**Key characteristics**
- Driven by other actors' strategic choices — politics, regulators, rivals
- No dataset exists; the future is decided, not revealed
- Probabilities would be invented, then mistaken for analysis

**The right tools**
- Scenario construction (worlds)
- Robustness testing across worlds
- Minimax regret instead of expected value

**In this case:** Will sovereign governments still pay a premium for non-US infrastructure in 2032? That depends on elections, alliances, and incumbents' concessions — decisions not yet made, by actors who don't yet know themselves.

---

## Level 4 · True ambiguity — unknown unknowns

**The question:** How do we stay adaptable?

Even the futures can't be enumerated; no scenario set is credible. Novel technology, novel markets and novel regulation interact in ways no one can pre-state. The only defensible posture is to design the decision itself for adaptation.

**Key characteristics**
- Futures cannot be listed, let alone weighted
- Multiple novelties interacting
- Resolves only by acting, observing, adapting

**The right tools**
- Signposts & triggers
- Staged commitment, options to exit
- Adaptive pathways — act, monitor, re-plan

**In this case:** What does the orbital economy look like in 2040 — debris regimes, mega-constellation shakeouts, direct-to-device upending terrestrial telcos? We cannot even list the candidate futures.

---

## The wall — why the move from level 2 to level 3 changes everything

**Level 2 · buy information**
The uncertainty lives **in our heads**. The world already holds an answer — a BOM cost, a launch quote, an MNO's willingness to sign — and study reveals it. More data shrinks the error bars. The right move is to analyze harder. The memo's own Phase-1 budget ($1.5–2.5M over 18 months: block-buy pricing, LOIs, BOM studies) is precisely this — and it is the correct instrument for these uncertainties.

**Level 3 · buy robustness**
The uncertainty lives **in the world**. The answer does not exist yet — sovereignty politics in 2032, the structure of the launch market — it will be *decided*, over years, by actors who haven't chosen. More analysis adds only invented precision. The right move is to stop forecasting and start stress-testing: find the decision that survives every plausible future, and instrument the ones it doesn't.

> The most common failure in strategy work is treating level 3 as level 2: putting made-up probabilities on scenarios and averaging them into one confident number. The math looks rigorous; the inputs are guesses wearing suits. **Once probabilities can't be defended, expected value stops being an honest summary — and robustness has to replace it.**

> Applied here: the Samsung verdict turns on uncertainties no Phase-1 study can resolve — sovereignty politics and launch-market structure. Better information stops helping at the level-2/3 boundary. **That is the wall, and the opportunity:** the analysis that follows is what working *on the far side* of it looks like.
